# Write a video script

1. Read the selected style guide and its evidence. Separate the news hook from the episode question. Pick a question that still makes sense after the launch news is removed. For a single request spanning multiple styles, choose distinct questions and story engines rather than drafting one outline twice. For an unspecified Chinese medium-length video, aim for about five minutes and budget time for on-screen action.
2. Outline the visible hook, the first plausible answer, the evidence that changes it, and what the viewer gets at the end. For a mechanism-led story, make the comparison explicit and use sources to change a judgment. For a task-led story, state what will be attempted, which variables or materials change, what counts as an outcome, and how the result decides the next move. Use the selected guide to decide what is said, what is shown, and which parts stay as silence or reaction.
3. Find and verify the evidence before finalizing the spoken lines. Keep a source trail mapping factual claims to primary sources. Distinguish document dates from event dates, company claims from observed outcomes, and plans from deliveries. Write only observed results as results. For work not yet run, supply a shootable protocol or honest conditional branches. Identify reenactments, props, and illustrative numbers where relevant.
4. Draft in the user's language with an original opening, specific spoken transitions, and a payoff that answers the opening. Mark narration, live lines, and production notes separately when they differ. Preserve the user's voice and avoid copying a channel's branding or signature lines. Keep source notes outside the spoken text.
5. Read the spoken text for timing and flow. Check that the first sequence can be filmed, at least one piece of evidence changes the story, and the ending adds a usable result or judgment. If two drafts could trade their middle sections, sharpen their distinct questions or evidence. Save the draft in the workspace's designated output directory. Publish it elsewhere only when requested.
